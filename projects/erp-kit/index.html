Go-live enablement kit: Sales coordinators on a new ERP

A compact, working example of the package a training lead builds for a system rollout: who needs what, how it is sequenced, what people keep on their desk, how they practise, and how readiness is measured.

Project brief

Marlow Distribution Co. (fictional, about 120 staff) is replacing spreadsheets and email with a single ERP. The first go-live covers order-to-cash.

Situation
Orders are taken by phone and email, retyped into three spreadsheets, and often shipped late or invoiced wrong.
Audience
6 sales coordinators. Experienced with the products and customers, comfortable with Excel, no ERP experience. Busy mornings, quieter after 2 pm.
Go-live
New orders enter the system on day one. Old spreadsheets go read-only. Support is on the floor for the first two weeks.
My role
Training lead: analysis, plan, job aids, practice, readiness measurement.

How success is measured

  • Before go-live: practice completion and readiness-check score (80% to be released to live entry).
  • First 4 weeks: order-entry errors per 100 orders, support tickets by cause, time to enter an order.
  • Action: a cause that appears in 3 or more tickets becomes a guide update or a micro-lesson.

Quick reference guide

The one-page job aid that sits next to the keyboard. It follows the task, not the menu.

Enter a customer order

  1. Go to Sales → New order.
  2. Pick the customer from the list. Do not type a new one. If it is missing, call the data desk.
  3. Click Add line, choose the product, set quantity.
  4. Check payment terms. They come from the customer record: change nothing without approval.
  5. Click Confirm order. The warehouse sees it only after this step.

Common mistakes
• Saving as draft and walking away: the warehouse never sees the order.
• Retyping an existing customer: creates a duplicate and splits their history.

Order wrong after confirming? Open it from Sales → Orders, click Edit if status is Confirmed. If status is Shipped, raise a ticket with your team lead.

Review checklist used on every AI draft

  • Steps match the process agreed with the business, not a generic one
  • No invented system behaviour, field names or numbers
  • One performance objective per piece of content
  • Reading level suits a busy coordinator, not a manual
